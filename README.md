NCCN Guideline Panel Simulator — Haematologic Malignancy Knowledge Reference

This document is the authoritative reference for the NCCN Guideline Panel Simulator agent. It contains verified historical precedents from NCCN B-Cell Lymphoma panel transparency documents (2021–2024), empirically derived calibration data, and sourced process information. Every precedent in this document is traceable to a specific NCCN source document.

Scope: This knowledge base covers haematologic malignancies with a deep focus on B-cell lymphoma panel decisions. The patterns and calibration anchors are derived from 93 real panel decisions (68 inclusions, 5 rejections and 20 removals of existing regimens) documented in 20 NCCN transparency files from 2021–2024. These are all of the B-Cell Lymphoma transparency documents that were publicly available for that period.

Section 1: NCCN Panel Process
Panel Meeting Structure
Panels meet for annual updates (Version 1.YYYY) and interim updates (V.2, V.3, etc.) throughout the year.
Interim meetings are held for new FDA approvals, expanded indications, or major new data from medical meetings.
At least 50% panel attendance is required for quorum.
Panel leadership (Chair and Vice-Chair) moderate meetings, set agendas, and enforce conflict-of-interest recusals.
Evidence Submission
External submissions must be received at least 4 weeks prior to the scheduled panel meeting.
All external submissions are made publicly available on the NCCN website.
A transparency document is published with each guideline update capturing: changes made, submission requests, a short summary of the panel discussion and rationale, references, and aggregate voting results.
Conflict of Interest
Panel members with a meaningful conflict of interest are recused from deliberations and/or votes.
Financial limits for panel chairs and members are $20,000 from a single entity and $50,000 in aggregate. Chairs and vice-chairs may not take part in marketing-focused activities.
Funding of clinical research activities is excluded from these limits.
Panels include a patient advocate as a full member whenever possible.
Categories of Evidence and Consensus
Category 1: Based on high-level evidence, there is uniform NCCN consensus (≥85% support of the panel) that the intervention is appropriate.
Category 2A: Based on lower-level evidence, there is uniform NCCN consensus (≥85% support of the panel) that the intervention is appropriate.
Category 2B: Based on lower-level evidence, there is NCCN consensus (≥50% but <85% support of the panel) that the intervention is appropriate.
Category 3: Based on any level of evidence, there is major NCCN disagreement that the intervention is appropriate (inclusion requires at least 25% support).
Most NCCN recommendations are Category 2A unless otherwise indicated.

Section 4: Decision Pattern Analysis (Derived from Verified Data)
Pattern 1: FDA-Approved Drug with Accelerated Approval (Single-Arm Data)

Observed behaviour:
When an FDA-approved therapy with accelerated approval (often based on single-arm data) is reviewed by the NCCN B-cell lymphoma panel, it is typically incorporated into the guidelines despite lower-level evidence, particularly in relapsed/refractory settings with high unmet need.

Typical outcome:
Most commonly Category 2A (lower-level evidence with uniform NCCN consensus).
Preference designation (preferred vs other recommended) varies depending on available alternatives, comparative evidence, and clinical experience.

Examples from database:

Epcoritamab (accelerated approval; EPCORE NHL-1 single-arm study): Category 2A
Glofitamab (accelerated approval; single-arm study): Category 2A
Pirtobrutinib (accelerated approval; BRUIN single-arm study): Category 2A
Loncastuximab tesirine (accelerated approval; single-arm study): Category 2A

Interpretation:
This pattern indicates that NCCN panels frequently consider single-arm evidence sufficient for clinical inclusion (Category 2A), even where confirmatory randomised evidence is not yet available.

Pattern 2: Category Upgrade After Confirmatory Phase III Data

Observed behaviour: Drugs initially included at Category 2A based on single-arm or early data are upgraded to Category 1 when phase III RCT data confirms benefit.

Examples from database:

Liso-cel in 2L DLBCL: Initially Cat 2A (2022) → Upgraded to Cat 1 after TRANSFORM phase III (2023). Vote increased from 87.5% to 95.8%.
Pola-R-CHP in 1L DLBCL: Cat 1, Preferred at 89.7% based on POLARIX phase III.
Axi-cel in 2L DLBCL: Cat 1 at 78.3% based on ZUMA-7 phase III.

Observed upgrade timeline: about 7 months in the one complete case in this database (liso-cel, 2A in June 2022 to Category 1 in February 2023), triggered by publication of the primary phase III analysis.

Pattern 3: Off-Label Inclusion with High Consensus

Observed behaviour: The B-cell lymphoma panel is comfortable including therapies off-label when clinical rationale is strong.

Examples from database:

Acalabrutinib for MZL: 100% vote, Preferred — no specific FDA approval for MZL
Pirtobrutinib for MZL: 100% vote, Preferred — no FDA approval in MZL
R-mini-CHOP for HGBL in frail/elderly: 100% vote, Cat 2A — clinical consensus without RCT
Pattern 4: Category 2B — Genuine Panel Disagreement

Observed behaviour: Category 2B is assigned when the vote falls between 50% and 84% (NCCN definition). Two 2B decisions in this database were below 50% (RCEPP, 48.3% and 44.8%). In the database, 7 decisions received 2B with an average vote of 56%.

Examples from database:

Liso-cel for DLBCL 2L (relapsed >12mo): 70.8% — 2B (later upgraded to 2A)
Venetoclax/lenalidomide/rituximab for MCL: 58.6% — 2B
Pola-R-CHP for HGBL double-hit: 56% — 2B (panel divided on extrapolation)
Alternate BTKi substitution in TRIANGLE: 58.6% — 2B (no RCT data for substitution)
RCEPP for DLBCL in frail elderly: 48.3% and 44.8% — 2B (both category downgrades from 2A)
Alternate BTKi + rituximab maintenance after HDT/ASCR in MCL: 55.2% — 2B

Key insight (interpretation): 2B commonly arises when evidence is being extrapolated from one population to another (e.g., specific drug to class substitution, one histology to another) without direct supporting data.

Pattern 5: Preference Level Dynamics

Observed behaviour from 68 inclusions:

Preferred: 11 decisions (16.2%)
Other Recommended: 11 decisions (16.2%)
Useful in Certain Circumstances: 1 decision (1.5%)
Not specified/N/A: 45 decisions (66.2%)

Drivers of "Preferred" status:

Phase III data showing superiority over existing SOC (Pola-R-CHP in 1L DLBCL: Cat 1, Preferred)
Bispecific antibodies in transformed DLBCL (high unmet need, novel mechanism)
BTK inhibitors with better safety profiles replacing older agents (zanubrutinib, acalabrutinib replacing ibrutinib)

Requested versus granted (observed): Companies often ask for more than the panel gives. Requests for Preferred were granted as Other Recommended for umbralisib (FL and MZL, February 2021), zanubrutinib + obinutuzumab (FL, 2023) and acalabrutinib + rituximab (MCL, 2023). A request for Category 2A was granted as 2B for liso-cel (2L DLBCL, relapse >12 months, 2022), and a request for Category 1 was first deferred and then refused before being granted. A logged "inclusion" therefore does not mean the company received what it asked for.

Drivers of "Other Recommended" status:

FDA-approved but without head-to-head superiority data vs existing options
Drugs with concerning toxicity profiles relative to alternatives
Section 5: B-Cell Lymphoma Panel Behavioural Profile

Derived from 93 verified decisions, 2021–2024

Panel Characteristics
Panel size: 31–34 voting members (varies by meeting; 0–11 absent per vote, so 20–34 votes cast)
Meeting frequency: Annual meeting plus interim updates as needed. The highest guideline version number reached was 5 in 2021, 5 in 2022 and 6 in 2023.
Voting patterns: Strong tendency toward consensus. 60% of all decisions (56 of 93) had ≥85% yes and 54% (50 of 93) had ≥90% yes.
Rejection rate: 5.4% across all decisions, but every rejection was an external-only request. Among external-only requests the rate was 27.8% (5 of 18). The panel rarely rejects items it raises itself.
Behavioural Tendencies
Comfortable with single-arm data: Bispecific antibodies (epcoritamab, glofitamab), pirtobrutinib, loncastuximab and CAR-T therapies in later lines were included at Category 2A on single-arm data.
Rapid responder: Epcoritamab was added 6 days after FDA approval and glofitamab 14 days after, consistent with the published 19-day median. In five cases the panel acted before FDA approval (see Section 2).
Off-label comfort: High. The panel included acalabrutinib and pirtobrutinib in MZL without an FDA indication, both as Preferred with unanimous votes, and included five therapies ahead of FDA approval. See Section 8, caveat 13.
Safety-conscious: Ibrutinib was unanimously downgraded from Preferred to Other Recommended in both MZL and MCL (29-0-0 each). The panel cited head-to-head trials in other B-cell malignancies showing a more favourable toxicity profile for acalabrutinib and zanubrutinib without compromising efficacy.
Extrapolation caution: Panel is comfortable with a drug in its approved indication but more cautious when extrapolating to different histologies or substituting one drug for another within a class without RCT data (see TRIANGLE BTKi substitution: 2B at 58.6%).
Category upgrade track record: Willing to upgrade categories when confirmatory data arrives (liso-cel 2A→1, Pola-R-CHP to Cat 1).
Section 6: Evidence Quality Evaluation Framework
Study Design Assessment
Strong: Phase III RCT, well-powered, active comparator
Moderate: Phase II RCT, large single-arm trial (n>100)
Weak: Phase I, small single-arm (n<50), retrospective, case series
Primary Endpoint Hierarchy (Haematologic Malignancies)
OS remains the gold standard but is difficult to demonstrate in indolent NHL
PFS is the most common phase III endpoint and is well-accepted by panels
ORR + CR rate are the basis for most accelerated approvals; panels accept these when responses are durable
CR rate and MRD negativity carry particular weight in haem-onc (more so than solid tumours)
DOR: Median DOR ≥12 months is generally considered durable in aggressive lymphomas (interpretive; no official NCCN threshold exists)
Effect Magnitude Thresholds (Interpretive — Not Official NCCN Thresholds)

Note: NCCN does not publish specific numerical thresholds for effect magnitude. The following are interpretive ranges derived from the patterns observed in the decision database and general oncology practice. Use as rough guidance only.

Strong: ORR ≥60%, CR ≥30%, PFS HR ≤0.70
Moderate: ORR 40–60%, CR 15–30%, PFS HR 0.70–0.80
Weak: ORR <40%, CR <15%, PFS HR >0.80

Section 8: Important Caveats
This database covers B-cell lymphoma panel decisions (2021–2024). Other haem-onc panels (AML, CLL, myeloma) may have different behavioural patterns, though the categorical framework is identical.
Voting percentages do not always align with category assignments. The panel may assign Cat 1 with <85% vote if the evidence itself is high-level (see axi-cel ZUMA-7: 78.3% vote, Cat 1). Conversely, Cat 2A can be assigned at votes below 85% (see axi-cel in MZL: 73.9% vote, Cat 2A).
Preference changes can pass at lower thresholds. Tafasitamab + lenalidomide achieved Preferred at 51.7%. Preference-level votes appear to have different dynamics than new inclusion votes.
Panel composition varies by meeting. Absent members ranged from 0 to 11 across votes, materially affecting vote percentages.
The B-cell panel is among the most active NCCN panels, with 4–6 guideline versions per year in this database. Other panels may update less frequently.
This is a simulation, not a prediction. Actual panel decisions involve clinical judgment, interpersonal dynamics, and real-time discussion that cannot be fully captured.
Cost considerations are not formally part of category assignments but are beginning to influence preference designations in some panels (notably CML).
NCCN Compendium listing follows guideline recommendations and is critical for off-label reimbursement coverage by CMS and commercial payers.
The overall rejection rate understates the risk for a company submission. All five rejections were external-only requests (5 of 18). Use the external-only rate when the scenario is a company submission that the panel has not raised itself.
Vote percentages for removals are not comparable with inclusions. In some removal entries the vote was on continued inclusion (a low yes vote led to removal); in others it was on the removal itself (a high yes vote). The 20 removals are excluded from the inclusion rates above.
The transparency documents give a short panel note with each vote, not the discussion. Reasons quoted in this document come from those notes. Where a reason is marked as an interpretation, the note does not state it.
"Included" does not mean the request was granted in full. See "Requested versus granted" in Pattern 5.
FDA status is recorded as it stood when the panel voted. Several therapies were included before FDA approval of that indication (axi-cel in 2L DLBCL, Pola-R-CHP in 1L DLBCL, liso-cel in FL and MCL, epcoritamab in FL), and axi-cel was included in MZL without an FDA indication. The reliable anchor for calibration is whether the panel entry cites a recent FDA approval, not whether the product is FDA-approved in general.
